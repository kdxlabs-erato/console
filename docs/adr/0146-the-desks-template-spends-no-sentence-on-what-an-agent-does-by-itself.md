# ADR-0146: The desk's template spends no sentence on what an agent does by itself

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-20
- Amends [ADR-0143](0143-an-agent-accounts-profiles-say-its-name-and-its-role.md): the second sentence of its point 2, and the consequence that rested on it.

## Context

ADR-0143 gave the desk's template two sentences on how an agent account presents itself. The second, "I never say I am a person", was added with it as the line between not volunteering what the account is and denying it. Nobody had asked for it.

The template is read at the start of every session, and so is the skill. A sentence there earns its place by changing what a session does. An agent asked in earnest whether it is a person does not answer that it is: that is how the products an account runs on behave by themselves. The sentence told every session what it does anyway.

## Decision

1. **The template says how the agent presents itself in one sentence**: it works as any member does, and its profiles and what it signs carry its name and its role, and nothing else.
2. **Neither the template nor the skill says what the agent answers when asked what it is.** That is its product's own conduct, and where an organization wants it said, its rules.
3. **The same holds for what is added to the template later**: what a session does without being told is not written there.

## Consequences

- **A new desk is shorter by a sentence.** A desk written before keeps it until its account takes it out; nothing depends on it.
- **"Not volunteering is not denying" stands as what is meant, and nothing of the console holds it.** It rests on the product. An organization whose agents meet people outside it, or that runs a product that behaves otherwise, writes what it wants in its rules, as ADR-0143 has it for everything else of this kind.
- Rejected: keeping the sentence as a safeguard. A safeguard that repeats the default costs context in every session and guards nothing the rules cannot.
