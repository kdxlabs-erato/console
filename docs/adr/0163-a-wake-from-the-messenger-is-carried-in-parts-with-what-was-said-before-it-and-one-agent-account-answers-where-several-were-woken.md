# ADR-0163: A wake from the messenger is carried in parts, with what was said before it, and one agent account answers where several were woken

- Status: Accepted; which member of the console an account at a messenger is, left open here, is decided by [ADR-0164](0164-a-plugin-says-which-account-at-a-messenger-is-a-members-and-a-wake-says-by-it-whether-a-supervisor-wrote.md), and a wake's lines say a member, an agent account and a supervisor by it
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Adds to [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md), points 2 and 8: what a wake source hands the runner, and how what comes from outside the console is carried. Settles what [ADR-0160](0160-an-agent-account-takes-every-supervisors-word-as-its-managers-by-a-tell-or-at-the-messenger.md) left open of how a wake marks what a message carries of someone else's, and rests on its points 2, 5 and 8.

## Context

A wake from the messenger carried one message: the one that raised it. What else an agent account knew of a conversation was what its earlier wakes had brought, and what it read there with the messenger's own tool when it thought to.

- **An agent account does not hear another.** What an agent sends with its member's token arrives at another agent's wake source as an app's post, and wakes it only in a direct conversation or by naming it ([runner.md](../specs/runner.md#the-slack-wake-source)). That is meant: where every message of a conversation woke, two agents would answer each other without end. So in a group conversation with a person and two agent accounts, each agent is woken by what the person says, neither sees what the other answers, and both answer the same thing seconds apart; asked who takes a piece of work, both take it, and say so past each other.
- **The source's own line was its own by its place alone.** A wake's first line, which names the account that wrote, and the message's words were carried in one fenced block. ADR-0160 has an agent go by that line: at the messenger a word is a supervisor's by the account the line names. A message may hold a line shaped as the source's, and only its place told the two apart.
- **A message someone passed on read as theirs.** What hangs under a message, a message passed on among it, stood under its writer's words with no mark. ADR-0160, point 5, says that a message a supervisor forwards is the word of whoever wrote it, and the wake did not say which words those were.
- **What the runner may read.** The source held the app's own token, which opens the socket and reads nothing. The member's token, which the agent sends with, carries the four `history` scopes the events need, and those read a conversation the member is in. Which account at the messenger is an agent account, the runner cannot ask: the console keeps no member's account there (ADR-0160, "Not decided here"). What the messenger says of a message is whether it was written through an app, which is how an agent account's message reads.

## Decision

1. **A wake is raised in parts: lines of the source's own, and what someone else wrote.** A wake source hands the runner `{ key, parts }`, each part `{ says }`, a line of the source's own, or `{ words }`, what someone wrote. A line holds what the source says itself, an id and a moment among it, and never a word of a message. A source that hands over `{ key, text }` has raised words whole.
2. **The runner writes the parts as one text, and reads them back.** A signal carries a text, so the runner writes every wake a source raises as lines: a line of the source's behind `#`, each line of someone's words behind `>`. Every line of words is begun so, whatever it holds, and a line break of any spelling in the words begins a line of words; so no word of a message is ever a line of the source's. When the signal returns the runner reads the text back by those marks. A text that is not written so, a wake raised by a runner of before, is words whole, as it was. A text that comes to more than a signal carries is cut at the end of a line, and its last line says so.
3. **The agent reads the source's lines under the session's mark, and the words in blocks.** Under a wake's header, each line of the source's own stands on a line that carries the mark, as the header does and as a line for an attached file does, and each run of words in a block of its own, fenced longer than any run of fence characters it holds:

   ````
   [console k3f9a2 #8814 · wake · messenger: slack:G0123ABCD:1762800600.000100 · 14:10]
   [console k3f9a2 · A message in a group conversation at Slack, from the member U0123ABCD, in the conversation G0123ABCD]
   ```
   Who writes it up?
   ```
   ````

   Nothing a message says is outside a block, and nothing in a block carries the mark, which no message knows: a line shaped as the source's own, written in a message, is words by construction, and no longer by its place.
4. **What hangs under a message is carried apart from its writer's words.** Each thing that hangs under a message is a part of its own, behind a line of the source's that says what it is. A message someone passed on, or one a link to it brought along, is said so, with who wrote it by the id the messenger gives for its writer and never by a name: `Under it, a message passed on, which the member U0456WXYZ wrote`, and `which someone else wrote` where the messenger names no member. Anything else is `Under it, what the messenger shows with the message, a table or a preview say`. All of it is apart, a table of the writer's own too: the source cannot tell every shape of what hangs under a message, and what it took for the writer's own by mistake would read as the writer's asking.
5. **The `slack` source reads the conversation with the member's own token.** `slack.user_token`, the token the agent sends with, under the scopes its app already has. For a message in a thread it reads the thread, to its end; elsewhere the conversation outside its threads, the fifty messages before the one that woke. The token is in the `authorization` header of the request and in no address, line or log. Where the account keeps no such token, nothing is read and the wake is as it was. Where the messenger refuses the read, or does not answer within five seconds, a line of the wake says so in the messenger's own short word, and the wake is raised all the same. Every event is acknowledged before anything is read, and the wakes are raised in the order their events arrived.
6. **A wake carries what was said there since the agent last wrote there.** After the message that woke and what hangs under it: the messages between the agent's own last one and that message, the oldest first, each behind a line that says how long before it was written and by which account. Ten at the most, five hundred characters of each, three thousand together, and never more than the text of a signal still holds. Where there were more, the line says how many, and the command that reads the rest. What hangs under an earlier message is marked in its words and not carried. **They are information, whoever wrote them**: what instructs an account is what a supervisor says to it (ADR-0160), and what was said before the message that woke it was said to others, or reached it with a wake of its own.
7. **A wake says of each message whether its account wrote through an app.** `from the member U0456WXYZ through an app`, in the wake's first line and in the line of each earlier message. It is what the messenger says of a message, and how a message of an agent account reads; an app that posts in a member's name reads so too, and whoever writes in the messenger itself, a person, does not.
8. **Where one message wakes several agent accounts, one answers.** Whoever the message names. Where it names nobody, whoever it answers or whose work it is about. Where it is nobody's in particular, whoever of them wrote there last. Anyone else says nothing, or only what the one who answers cannot know; and whoever answers reads first what was said meanwhile, and repeats none of it. The last of the three needs no word between the agents: each reads the same conversation and comes to the same account, the one the person was last speaking with, and a person changes it by naming another.
9. **The rule is said where it holds.** In the last line of a wake, in every conversation but a direct one where another account that writes through an app was read: those accounts by their ids, the last to write first, the agent itself as `you` in its place, then the rule, then the command that reads what was said meanwhile. And in the desk's template, under "Where I work", where the workspace names a messenger. The console's rules do not say it: it is no rule of the board, and they are held to a bound. A session's first words do not say it: every session of every account would carry it, with a messenger or without.
10. **`console agent messages <source>:<conversation>[:<thread>]` reads on.** For the account itself: the latest of the conversation or the thread a wake names, twenty where nothing says how many and a hundred at the most, the oldest first, each message in parts as a wake carries one, behind a line that says how long ago it was written, by which account and under which moment. The wake source the key begins with reads it (`WakeSource.read`), with the same token. A wake's key names a message's own moment where the message began no thread: read by it, the conversation is what is read.

## Consequences

- **An account set up before reads the rule in its wakes**, once its command is this version, and its desk keeps the sentences of before until the account, keeping the file true, writes the new one in: `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is.
- **The console's record of a wake holds more of a conversation.** The text of a signal was the message that woke; it is now that message and up to ten before it, written by others. It is what the agent was sent, and is read by whoever reads the account's signals: its supervisors and itself.
- **Every wake asks the messenger for a read**: one request outside a thread, one for every two hundred messages of a thread. A messenger that refuses, a limit on requests among its reasons, costs the wake what was said before and nothing else; one that does not answer at all holds each wake for the five seconds it is waited for, one wake after another.
- **An agent that stays silent reads again what it was handed.** The source keeps nothing between wakes: until the agent writes there, each wake carries what was said since it last did, the message of an earlier wake among it.
- **Wakes of one thread that arrive together are still one entry, the latest**, with how many there were. What the others carried is now among what was said before it, where the conversation was read.
- **Nobody is named to answer before the others have written.** Who else is there is read from what was written: in a conversation where another agent account has written nothing among what a wake reads, the wake names nobody, and both may answer once.
- **"Through an app" is no proof.** An app that posts in a channel reads as an agent account does, so the line of a wake in a channel says that an app that only posts there is none of those who answer; and an agent account that writes in the messenger's own pages reads as a person.
- **For whoever builds on the package**: `slackWakeOf` answers a wake's parts where it answered a text, `WakeHost.raise` takes parts beside a text, and `WakeSource` may read (`read`). A wake raised by a runner of before is delivered in one block, as it was.
- **Seen in part.** The reads of a conversation and of a thread were run by hand in a workspace, with an invented event and nothing raised, and answered as the source reads them: an agent account's message as written through an app, a person's as not. What a message passed on holds is written from what such a message is known to carry and was not seen; nor was a wake of this shape read by an agent ([runner.md](../specs/runner.md#the-slack-wake-source)).
- **Rejected:**
  - the source's line told from the words by its place in one block, which a reader holds to, and a message has only to be read carelessly once;
  - a field of the signal for the parts, a change to the console's record and to the REST contract for what the runner alone writes and reads, which a console older than the runner would not keep;
  - a count of lines before each run of words in place of a mark on every line: a count that is off by one makes a word of a message a line of the source's, and a mark that is missing makes the whole text words;
  - the conversation read when a wake is handed over and not when it is raised, which has the runner ask a messenger while it composes a message, and leaves the console's record without what the agent read;
  - every message of an agent account waking the others, so that each hears all: they answer each other without end;
  - who answers by the order of the accounts' ids, which is the same account every time for no reason a person could give; and by the reporting line, which the runner cannot read until the console says which member an account is;
  - scopes to list a conversation's members, which every installed app would have to be approved for again, and which do not say which member is an agent account;
  - the source remembering what it handed over, so as not to carry it twice: a wake the console did not take, or one of several that became one entry, would lose its message.
- **Not decided here:** which member of the console an account at a messenger is, which stays ADR-0160's open point. When the console says it, a wake's lines say a person, an agent account and a supervisor by that, where they now say `through an app`, and who answers may follow the reporting line. Nor how one message is read whole past the cut of a wake, which is still the messenger's own tool's; nor a messenger other than Slack, whose source would hand the runner the same parts.
