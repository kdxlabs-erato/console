# ADR-0164: A plugin says which account at a messenger is a member's, and a wake says by it whether a supervisor wrote

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Settles what [ADR-0160](0160-an-agent-account-takes-every-supervisors-word-as-its-managers-by-a-tell-or-at-the-messenger.md) and [ADR-0163](0163-a-wake-from-the-messenger-is-carried-in-parts-with-what-was-said-before-it-and-one-agent-account-answers-where-several-were-woken.md) left open, "who a member of the messenger is in the console", and adds to [ADR-0050](0050-plugin-code-runs-in-a-host-process-of-its-own-and-reaches-the-console-only-through-the-capabilities-it-declared.md)'s catalogue of capabilities. Rests on ADR-0160, points 2 and 8, on ADR-0163, points 1 to 3, and on [ADR-0084](0084-an-agent-account-is-a-member-of-its-own-with-a-manager-above-it.md), point 5.

## Context

An agent account takes a supervisor's own word at the messenger as an instruction (ADR-0160). A wake from the messenger named the account that wrote by the messenger's id for it and said nothing of whose it is: the console kept no member's account at a messenger. So the agent was left to know it for itself, by the address the messenger keeps for the account or by a supervisor's tell that names it, and every agent did that work again, each as well as it could.

Who is matched to which account is known in one place. A plugin that carries the console's notifications to a messenger matches each member to their account there by the work email, which neither side lets its holder set freely, and keeps the match in its own records. Nothing passed it on.

Whoever can say that an account is a member's can make an agent account believe that a supervisor wrote. That is what this decision weighs.

## Decision

1. **A plugin says which account is a member's, under a capability of its own.** `people:accounts` opens `ctx.console.accounts`: `put({ personId, messenger, workspace, account })`, `remove({ personId, messenger, workspace })` and `list({ messenger, workspace })`. The messenger is named as a wake source is (`slack`); the workspace there and the account by the messenger's ids for them, held to the shape of an id. No other capability opens it, `people:read:all` least of all, and it opens nothing else: an administrator consents to it by name, reading that an agent account takes a supervisor's word by it.
2. **The console keeps what was said, with who said it.** `messenger_accounts`: one member for an account, and one account of a member in a workspace of a messenger. What a plugin said before of the account or of the member there gives way to what it says now. What another plugin said of either stands, and the call is refused (`plugin.conflict`): no plugin says over another. The member is active when it is said. A workspace keeps so many, of every messenger together.
3. **What was said counts only while the plugin that said it is enabled and holds the capability.** Read at the moment the console is asked: a plugin disabled, waiting for consent or with the capability taken back has said nothing, and its rows stand unread until it holds the capability again. A member who is deactivated is nobody.
4. **Every change is an event of the workspace under the plugin's name.** `person.updated` with the field `account`: the member's login, the messenger, the workspace and the account, and `set`, whether the account is theirs now or no longer, in the transaction that makes the change. It is said as a change of the member, and not as a kind of event of its own, so that a command of before reads the feed it is on ([ADR-0043](0043-the-rest-contract-is-held-to-a-snapshot-taken-at-the-floors.md)). The workspace's feed keeps them, so whoever reads it sees later when an account became a member's and by which plugin.
5. **An agent account asks the console who wrote, for itself.** `POST /api/v1/agents/me/writers`, on a session of the account's own: accounts at a messenger, a hundred at most, each answered with `told`, whether the console is told the accounts of that workspace at all, and `member`, the active member the account is, by login, with their kind and whether they supervise the account that asks. A supervisor is who ADR-0084 says: an administrator, or whoever is above the account in the reporting line. It is asked of nobody's supervisors but the asker's own, and by no person.
6. **A wake says it in the source's own line.** The `slack` source asks before it writes a wake, and after the account's id the line says `(@kim in the console, a supervisor of yours)`, `(@nova in the console, an agent account, no supervisor of yours)`, or `(no member the console knows, so no supervisor of yours)` for an account the console is told nothing of in a workspace it is told the accounts of. The line of each earlier message a wake carries says the same, and so does each line of `console agent messages`.
7. **No message can write it.** It stands in a line of the source's own, which the agent reads under its session's mark and which no word of a message reaches (ADR-0163, points 2 and 3). What it says is the console's answer to the account's own sign-in for that account in that workspace: the source asks by the id and the workspace the messenger's event names for the writer, never by the agent's own workspace where the event names none, hears only an answer for what it asked, and writes a login into the line only where it is shaped as one. A name the messenger shows, a title, a note and what a message says of its writer are not read for it.
8. **Where either side says nothing, a wake is as it was.** A console that does not know the question, one that fails or does not answer within five seconds, a workspace no plugin told it the accounts of, and a client or a source that asks nothing: the line names the account as the messenger does, and the agent knows a supervisor's account as ADR-0160, point 2, has it. A plugin that asks for the capability as one it can go without runs as before until an administrator turns it on.
9. **The agent goes by the line where it says, and is told so.** A session's first words, the desk's template and the skill say that the wake's line tells whose the account is where the console knows it, under the session's mark, and that the address the messenger keeps, or a supervisor's tell that names the account, tells it where the line says nothing.

## Consequences

- **A plugin that holds `people:accounts` can make an agent account take anyone's account at that messenger for a supervisor's.** That is the capability's reach, written in the catalogue and in the sentence an administrator consents to. What bounds it: the install path, the consent by name, the event for every change under the plugin's name, and the alarm of ADR-0160, point 1, which holds whoever asks. A match is as sure as what the plugin matched by: the messenger plugin matches by the work email, which the identity provider or an administrator wrote on the console's side and the workspace's administrators govern on the messenger's.
- **What a supervisor's account at the messenger carries is unchanged.** Whoever holds it instructs the accounts that supervisor supervises, as ADR-0160 says; the line makes the agent sure of whose account it is, not of who holds it.
- **The change is a people event.** A plugin that reads the directory (`people:read:all`) hears them with their content, as it hears who came and went: which member has which account at a messenger, for the plugins an administrator let read everyone.
- **A wake asks the console once, and once more where it carries earlier messages.** The account's own session asks, within the wait a read of the conversation has; a console that is slow costs the wake that line and nothing else.
- **An agent account's own account is said as one.** `an agent account` after a login is the console's word for what `through an app` only suggests. Who answers where several agents were woken still goes by who wrote last (ADR-0163, point 8).
- **For whoever builds on the package:** `WakeHost.writers` is there for a wake source to ask by, where the client has `writers`; `ctx.console.accounts` is left out by a console older than the capability, and a plugin that runs on both asks whether it is there.
- **Rejected:**
  - the console matching members to accounts itself, with a token of the messenger's kept on the server: the console holds no credential of a messenger's, and one plugin already does the matching under an administrator's consent;
  - members saying their own account: a member who names a supervisor's account as their own is then no supervisor, but one who names their own as a supervisor's would be, unless every claim were proved at the messenger, which is the plugin's work;
  - the capability folded into `people:read:email` or `people:read:all`: a plugin that reads addresses would then instruct agents, with nobody having consented to that;
  - the answer written into the wake by the console when the signal is raised: the runner writes a wake's text and reads it back by its marks (ADR-0163), and a console that rewrote it would be a second writer of what only the runner reads;
  - the member's name in the line, which is the member's own to write and would put words of theirs under the session's mark;
  - the agent's own workspace taken for the writer's where the event names none: the same id at another organization is another account, and the line would say of it what the console said of a colleague.
- **Not decided here:** who answers by the reporting line where one message wakes several agent accounts, which the console could now say; a page of the console that lists a member's accounts at a messenger, the events being what there is to read; and a messenger other than Slack, whose plugin and wake source would say and ask in the same way.
