# ADR-0157: The console decides who is told of what and keeps the record, and a plugin carries it out

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Supersedes in part [ADR-0071](0071-the-console-sends-no-notification-of-its-own-a-plugin-carries-them-out-of-the-board-and-back.md), under which the console sent no notification of its own and a plugin both chose whom to tell and told them: choosing whom is the console's now, and carrying it out of the console stays a plugin's. Amends point 10 of [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md): the board's wakes are what an agent account's notifications are sent as, and what an account is woken for is the account's to choose.

## Context

Two things told a member of what happened on their work, by two sets of rules. The board woke an agent account, by rules the worker applied to the feed. The messenger plugin told a person, and an agent account that has an account at the messenger, by rules of its own: whom to tell of a task, what each chose to be told of, and a record of what it sent that only whoever keeps the messenger reads.

An agent account was told twice, once by each. Twenty tasks commented on and closed in three minutes reached their owner, an agent account, as some forty messages, a wake and a direct message for each piece of news, and nothing let it say of one task that it wanted none. The same is true of a person: a task could be neither followed nor muted, in the console or in the messenger. And a person had no place in the console that said what they had been told: the setting was the messenger's while the work was the console's, so that what one is told of a task could be set on no page of the task, and a thing turned off in one place was sent from the other all the same.

ADR-0071 gave all of this to a plugin, for a reason that still holds for half of it: the words in a person's language, the thread a message belongs to and the button that answers are a messenger's to know. Who is concerned by what happened on the board is not: it is read from the board, and every carrier would work it out again by rules of its own.

## Decision

1. **The console decides who is told of what happens on the board.** The worker reads the feed from a cursor, as it did for the wakes, and makes a **notification** for each member an event concerns: one row for a member and an event, under the **kind** that says why. A person and an agent account are told by the same rules:

   | What happened | Who is told | Kind |
   |---|---|---|
   | A task written | its owner and those at work on it | `assigned`; `ready` for those at work on it where it is ready and not planned for a later day |
   | Someone put to work on a task | those the change added | `assigned`, or `ready` as above |
   | A ready task's day moved to today or before, or taken away | those at work on it | `ready` |
   | A task's title, body or days changed | its owner and those at work on it | `changed` |
   | A task moved from one state to another | its owner; those at work on it where it became ready, as above | `moved`; `ready` |
   | A comment on a task | its owner and those at work on it | `commented` |
   | A task put away | its owner and those at work on it | `archived` |
   | A decision raised, or addressed anew | whom it is for, those the change named | `decision` |
   | A decision answered | whoever raised it | `answered` |
   | An agent account stopped, refused, failed or not heard from, and running again after | whom it reports to | `agent` |
   | A decision an agent account raised for whom it reports to | that manager | `decision`, or `agent` where they have no decision on ([ADR-0158](0158-an-agent-account-holds-to-how-its-manager-set-it-up-tells-its-manager-of-what-tries-to-instruct-it-and-waits-for-a-word-before-it-spends.md)) |

   Nobody is told of what they did themselves; a task the console itself gave back, ending a run it had not heard from, tells nobody ([ADR-0114](0114-the-end-of-a-run-leaves-no-task-at-work-behind-it.md)); and a member is told only of what they may still see: a task of a project they may work in, a decision that reaches them.
2. **A kind is on or off for a member, and a notification is made only under a kind that is on.** Where an event is several kinds to a member, they are told once, under the first that is on, the one that says most first: `ready` before `assigned`. A member turns a kind on and off, turns a project off, and says whether what an agent of their own did as them counts, which it does not unless they say so. Where they chose nothing:
   - **a person is told of the agent accounts that report to them, and of nothing else.** What happens on their own work is on the board when they look, and what was not asked for is noise;
   - **an agent account is told of what asks something of it**: `ready`, `commented`, `decision`, `answered`, and `agent` for one that others report to. Nobody attends an agent account, so that what it is not told of it does not learn of. These are the events the board woke it for before.
3. **A task tells a member something or nothing, which a bell on its page says and turns.** It is on to begin with where the task is theirs, its owner's or a worker's, a kind they have on would go on telling them of it, and they did not turn its project off; and off for every other task. Turned on where it was off, the task tells them of everything on it, its changes, its moves, its comments and its being put away, whatever their kinds and whatever its project: it is the member saying so of that one task, and no kind of theirs would tell them. Turned off, it tells them nothing, its owner and its workers among them. Turned to what their settings say already, nothing is kept of the choice, so that a task turned off and on again tells them by their kinds as before. Anyone who may see a task may turn its bell; the command's words for the two are `follow` and `mute`.
4. **A notification is read in the console, by its member alone, under a bell in the head of every page.** The bell is marked while something is not read, and the list opens under it where the member is, the newest first. Opening it reads all of it at one moment, and what was read at that moment is shown as new that once: on the pages nothing is read one by one, and what is new is told by when it was read. A row says who did what in two lines at most, what it names cut short, with why, where and when under it, and leads to the thing. What a member chose to be told of is a view of the settings, theirs alone. `GET /api/v1/notifications` answers the list, each with the feed's own event, said as the feed says it, its project and when it was read; `POST .../read` marks those named, or all, and answers the moment it read them at; `/api/v1/me` counts those not read. A notification is answered only while its member may still see the thing it is of. It is kept ninety days: the record of what happened is the feed, and this is a member's list of it.
5. **For an agent account, a notification is its wake.** The signal on the account's record and the notification are made together or not at all, so that a pass that ends halfway tells nobody twice, and the notification is read as it is made: the wake said it, and an account's list is a record of what it was woken for, with nothing in it left to read. What an account turned off does not wake it, and a task it muted wakes it for nothing: the wake rules of ADR-0085 are these rules with the defaults of point 2. The console's own housekeeping, a change of the services a workspace names, wakes every account as before, and is no notification.
6. **Carrying a notification out of the console is a plugin's, and of what the console made, no more.** A plugin that holds the new capability `notifications:read` is handed, with each console event it hears, whom the console told of it: `data.told`, each `{ personId, kind, woken, followed }`. `woken` says the console told the member itself, by a wake of its agent account, so that the carrier carries that one to nobody; `followed` says they are told because they turned the task's bell on themselves, so that a carrier that keeps a member's choice by kind knows the member asked for this one task. A kind a member turned off makes no notification, but of a task whose bell they turned on themselves, and so no carrier sends it: what a member is told in the console is the most any plugin sends them, and `told` is that, the same list for every plugin that is handed it. Which of it one plugin sends is that plugin's own setting, and none of the console's: the plugin offers it on a page of its own and by a command of its own, an agent account choosing as a person does, and keeps it in its own records or tables, and the console's settings, which are of everything a member is told, name no plugin and keep nothing of one. The event itself reaches the plugin as it did, whoever was told of it and whoever was not: a plugin does more with an event than carry it, and one handed no event would stop keeping its own records where it should only stay quiet. Such a plugin hears an event once the board's pass has read it, a few seconds after it happened. How it is carried, at once or once a day, in which conversation, with which words and buttons, stays the carrier's, as ADR-0071 has it.
7. **A member chooses from the command as from the pages**: `console notifications`, which lists and marks as read; `console notify`, which turns kinds and projects; `console follow` and `console mute`, which turn a task on and off. Each is the caller's own, a person's agent with their token as the person ([ADR-0021](0021-a-token-configures-as-its-person-does.md)).

## Consequences

- **An agent account is woken for what it was woken for**, where it chose nothing, and its text is the one it read before. It may now turn a kind off, turn a task off, or have a kind wake it that did not: a task moved, changed or put away.
- **A person who chose nothing reads an empty list**, but for their agent accounts. The list says so and leads to the settings; a task's bell is the way to be told of one task without turning a kind on for all of them.
- **Until a messenger reads `told`, it tells by rules of its own**, and an agent account that has an account there may still be told twice of what both tell: the plugin's side is its repository's work. The capability and the list are what it needs for it, and a carrier that sends to nobody `told` does not name holds to what each member chose in the console without reading a setting of the console's.
- **A member has two places to choose in, and each is of one thing**: the console's settings, of what they are told at all, and a carrier's own, of what that carrier sends them of it. Turning a kind off in the console turns it off everywhere; a carrier's setting never tells a member of more than the console does. A carrier that offers no setting sends everything `told` names.
- **A console of before answers none of this.** The command says so of a console that has no such address, and the pages offer the list where `/api/v1/me` counts notifications, which an older console does not.
- **The worker's pass does a little more for each event**: it reads who turned the task on or off and what each member concerned chose. The members concerned by an event are a handful.
- **Two lists must agree**: the kinds a task goes on telling its owner and its workers of, by which a bell is on to begin with (`TASK_KINDS_OF`), and the table of point 1. A test holds them equal by doing everything that can happen to a task and reading what each was told of.
- **Rejected:** a notification as an event of the feed, which would put each member's list where a project's members read it; a page and a tab of their own for the list, which sends a member away from where they are for a list they look at for a moment; reading one by one, a press a row where opening the list says they saw it; three states for a task, by the settings, everything and nothing, which is a third thing to explain where a bell is on or off; a plugin that is handed no event of a kind a member turned off, which would silence what it does besides carrying, and for every member where one turned it off; a row for each carrier in the console's settings, with the kinds a member turned off for it kept by the console and left out of the list it is handed, which puts a plugin's setting where the plugin is not: what a carrier lets a member choose is more than kinds, at once or once a day, in which conversation, and a setting kept in two places is explained twice; a kind that is sent on and not listed, since what is sent would then be on no record a member reads; mail and push sent by the console, which ADR-0071 rejected and nothing here takes back; and a list of everything that concerns a member whatever they chose, which is the feed read by a filter.
- **Not decided here:** what a run does on a task, a report or what it hands in, which is told to nobody, whoever turned the task on among them; a decision about a task, which is told to whom it is for whatever they made of the task; a mention of a member in a comment, a stream by which a page learns of a notification the moment it is made (the count follows the board's feed and a visit to the page), and a supervisor choosing for an agent account what it is told of.
