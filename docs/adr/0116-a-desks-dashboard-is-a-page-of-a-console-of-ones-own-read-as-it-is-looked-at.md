# ADR-0116: A desk's dashboard is a page of a console of one's own, read as it is looked at

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-15
- Builds on [ADR-0014](0014-a-persons-own-console-is-served-from-their-machine-by-the-command.md) (a person's own console) and [ADR-0106](0106-a-look-is-a-part-with-a-file-and-a-row-and-a-page-places-its-parts.md) (the parts); the desk it belongs to is a member's own repository, an agent account's by [ADR-0088](0088-an-agent-account-has-a-desk-and-is-set-up-from-an-invitation-by-signing-in-as-itself.md) and a person's by [ADR-0115](0115-a-person-has-a-desk-too-written-by-the-command-and-set-up-by-a-skill-of-the-organizations-pack.md).

## Context

A person's desk is their own repository: their agent's instructions, their projects checked out, what should outlive a session. A person also wants to see their own work at a glance, across every project they are in: what stands now, how much they finished, what they closed. The organization's console answers the first on My work, by due day; the second and the third it answers a project at a time, on a board and in its archive.

Three shapes were open. The desk could keep a copy of the person's work, written by a script and read by a page: a second record of the board on a disk, in a repository that is pushed, and stale from the moment it is written. The organization's console could grow the pages for everyone: a product decision of its own, and nothing a person could change. Or the desk could hold a console of the person's own, which the package and `console serve` have made possible since ADR-0014, with one page more.

## Decision

1. **The dashboard is a console of the person's own with one page more.** The desk's `web/` is the four files of a console of one's own: the package's composition, `createConsole`, with `pages: [deskPage()]`. It is built there and served from the person's machine by `console serve`, which carries the API to the organization's console with the token the command keeps; the pages never see it. The page is at `/pages/desk`, and the shell opens on it.
2. **Nothing of the board is kept.** Every view is read from the console when it is looked at, and again each time it is come back to. The desk holds no copy of a task, a run or a decision, in a file or in the browser, so that what a private project holds is never in a repository that is pushed.
3. **Three views, each with an address.** What stands now: the open tasks the person works on by state, the sooner due first; the decisions that wait for their answer; their open runs. The figures: the tasks done by week over eight weeks, how their runs ended, and both by project and by category. The archive: the tasks they worked on that are done, dropped or put away, and their runs that ended, found by words of a title. The view and the words are the address's query (`?view=archive&q=...`, [ADR-0010](0010-the-default-uis-addresses-name-what-they-show-and-every-page-and-form-has-one.md)), which a page of a console's own is now given (`search` in what `render` receives).
4. **It reads through the routes there are, and what a visit reads is bounded.** No route was added. Now is the plate (`GET /api/v1/work?brief=true`), then one listing of open runs for each project where a task of the plate says a run is open. The figures and the archive are one page of each project: the closed tasks the person worked on (`open=false&worker=`), those put away (`archived=true&worker=`), `DESK_TASKS` of each, and the ended runs, `DESK_RUNS`, of which the person's are kept; three requests a project, `DESK_PROJECTS` projects at most, by name. Where a project has more, the figures say what they were counted from and the archive offers the next page; where a person may see more projects than a visit reads, the page says how many it read. The runs are narrowed by whom they are for on the page, since `mine=true` with a token is the runs begun with that token, which a person who signed their command in again would lose.
5. **It is built from the parts that exist.** Rows, facts, panes, badges, the tabs of a page's views: no part was added and nothing is drawn by the page. A figure is a fact, a name over a number as text. The views are components of the package (`DeskStanding`, `DeskFiguresView`, `DeskArchive`), each taking its data as props; `DeskPage` reads for them, and `deskPage()` is the page a console of one's own adds.
6. **The package ships the shell.** `template/desk-web` is those four files with the package's version and the desk's title left as words for the command that writes a desk to fill, the version in the manifest and the title in the page and the README alone, as text and never in a script; it names no console, no project and no token. A person changes it as any console of their own: a page of theirs is one more entry of `pages`.
7. **The organization's console says where a desk comes from, in one line.** On the Agents page, under how an agent is connected: the command that makes a desk, shown to be copied as the invitation's line is. It says what the command gives, and nothing of who runs it.

## Consequences

- A person's dashboard needs nothing of the deployment and shows nothing the person may not see: it asks as the person, with their token, and the console decides on every request.
- The figures are of what a visit read, not of the whole history: eight weeks, the latest hundred closed tasks of each kind and thirty runs of each project, twelve projects. They say so where a project had more. A figure that must be exact over a year is a report, read with a script against the API.
- A visit to the figures or the archive is three requests for each project read; a run in a listing carries its reports, which is why fewer runs are read than tasks. A listing of runs without their reports, or a person's closed work across projects in one answer, would make it one request; neither was added here.
- A page of a console's own can now be shown several ways by its address, since it is given the query.
- Rejected: a snapshot of the person's work written into the desk (a second record, stale, and the organization's work in a personal repository); the views as pages of the organization's console (a decision for everyone, where this is one person's own); a look of its own for the figures, a chart or a large numeral (a new part, which is a decision of design, asked for and not written).
