Breaking: a wake from the messenger is carried in parts, with what was said before it, and where one message wakes several agent accounts, one answers (ADR-0162). An agent account's message wakes no other unless it names it, so two agents in one group conversation each answered a person without seeing the other's answer. And a wake's first line, which names the account that wrote, stood in one block with the message's words.

- **The runner's own lines can no longer be written by a message.** Under a wake's header, each line of the wake source's own stands on a line that carries the session's mark (`[console k3f9a2 · A direct message at Slack, from the member U0123ABCD, in the conversation D0123ABCD]`), and what someone wrote in a block of its own. Before, the line and the words were one block, and the line was the source's by its place alone.
- **A message someone passed on is carried apart from the words of whoever passed it on**, behind a line that says who wrote it, by the messenger's id for the writer: `Under it, a message passed on, which the member U0456WXYZ wrote`. Whatever else hangs under a message, a preview or a table, is carried apart too. Before, it stood under the writer's words, unmarked.
- **A wake from Slack carries what was said there since the agent last wrote there**: ten messages at the most, five hundred characters of each, the oldest first, each behind a line that says how long before it was written and by which account, as information whoever wrote it. The source reads the conversation, or the thread, with the member's own token, `slack.user_token`, under the scopes the app already has. An account that keeps no such token is woken as before; where the messenger refuses the read, a line of the wake says so.
- **A wake says whether an account wrote through an app** (`from the member U0456WXYZ through an app`), which is how an agent account's message reads. Which member of the console an account is, a wake still does not say.
- **Who answers is said in the wake**, in every conversation but a direct one where another account that writes through an app was read: whoever the message names; where it names nobody, whoever it answers or whose work it is about; where it is nobody's in particular, whoever of them wrote there last; anyone else says nothing, or only what the one who answers cannot know. The desk's template says the same under "Where I work" where the workspace names a messenger; `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is, and its account writes the sentence in.
- **`console agent messages <source>:<conversation>[:<thread>]`** reads the latest of a conversation or a thread, as a wake's header names it after `messenger:`, for the account itself: where a wake carried less than is needed, and before answering where others may have answered meanwhile.
- **For whoever builds on `@skillcdn/console/runner`:** a wake source raises `{ key, parts }`, each part `{ says }`, a line of its own, or `{ words }`, what someone wrote (`WakePart`); `{ key, text }` is still taken, as words whole. `slackWakeOf` answers `parts` where it answered `text`. The runner writes every wake as one text, a line of the source's behind `#` and each line of words behind `>` (`wakeTextOf`, `wakePartsOf`), which is what the console's record of the signal now holds. A source may read a conversation for the command (`WakeSource.read`).

The reads of a conversation and of a thread are written from the messenger's documentation, tested against invented answers and were run by hand in a workspace; what a message passed on holds was not seen there, nor a wake of this shape read by an agent.
