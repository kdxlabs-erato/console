A runner no longer ends a session under the sub-agents at work in its background (ADR-0162). It took a session for idle once the agent's own turn was over, and ended it then for a model or an effort chosen, for the account's own reset (`console agent reset me`), for an update of the command and for a `runner.json` that changed. An agent account hands its tasks to sub-agents and ends its turn, so that is how it stands most of the time it works, and each of those ended the product's process and the sub-agents in it, in the middle of their work.

Each of them now waits until no sub-agent is at work in the background of the session, one that waits for a command of its own there included, as Claude Code lists them, and until the turn the product starts to hand the agent a sub-agent's report completed: an hour at most, after which the session ends as it would have. A `status` event of the session says once that the runner waits, with what for and how many sub-agents are at work, and once that it waited in vain. With an update seen, the runner goes on handing the agent what arrives while it waits for sub-agents. A day's or a week's briefing that would begin a new conversation (`conversation` in `runner.json`) is handed to the open session where sub-agents are at work, as where a turn is under way.

A supervisor's stop, restart and reset end the session at once, as before, and so does stopping the runner: to have a model taken now, send a restart after choosing it. A session of the `codex` or the `acp` driver, which say nothing of sub-agents in the background, ends as before, and so does one whose agent could not go on. `DriverEvent` has two words more, `background` (how many sub-agents are at work there) and `turn_started`: a driver of your own says them where its product does, and its sessions are held then. A runner built on the package's modules takes two more durations in `timing`, `subagentsMs` and `reportMs`; `SUBAGENTS_MS` and `REPORT_MS` are their defaults.
