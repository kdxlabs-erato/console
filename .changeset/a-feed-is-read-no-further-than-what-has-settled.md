The console reads its feeds no further than what has settled (ADR-0125). An event is numbered when it is written and seen when its change is committed, so a change numbered first could be committed last, and a reader that had gone past its number never read it: an agent account was not woken until the task's next event or its next briefing, a plugin's hook never heard of the event, and a page stayed stale until the next one.

- **Writers:** whatever writes an event holds a lock, shared with the other writers, until its change is committed. Writers never wait for each other, and never behind a reader. The lock is on a table of the console's own, one a feed, in a schema nobody else may use: a plugin with a schema of its own cannot hold the board's writes or its feeds by taking it.
- **Readers:** every read by number ends at the highest number below which no change is still under way: the board's wakes, the plugins' hooks on both streams, `GET .../events` with `after` or `latest=true`, the stream, a `--wait` for an answer, and `GET .../plugin-events`. An event committed while an earlier one is still under way is carried a moment later, in the order of the numbers.
- **`503 feed.unsettled`:** a process of the console that has not seen a feed settle since it began, while a change is still under way, answers `GET .../events` and `GET .../plugin-events` with `503`, `feed.unsettled` and `retry-after: 1`, and not with an empty page. The pages ask again by themselves, a second apart; a script that reads the feed should do the same, and never take the refusal for a feed with nothing in it. What the API answers is otherwise as it was.
- **A job no longer stops for good at one event:** an event whose wakes fail in twelve passes in a row is passed over, with an error in the log that names its number and kind; an event the console could not make ready for a plugin's hook is skipped after its tries with a `plugin.failed` event, as one whose hook fails is.

A migration adds the schema `feed_order`, its tables `events` and `plugin_events`, which hold no row, and its function `writers_ended`; the console's database user makes it, as it made the transcript's schema. Writers that follow each other without a gap, a load of well over a hundred events a second from several writers side by side, hold the feeds where they are until they end; the console says so in its log after thirty seconds.
