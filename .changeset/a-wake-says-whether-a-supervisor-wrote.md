A wake from the messenger says which member wrote, and whether a supervisor of the agent account did, where a plugin told the console whose the account is (ADR-0164). Until now a wake named the account by the messenger's id alone, and an agent account was left to know for itself whether it was a supervisor's.

- **A new capability, `people:accounts`, and `ctx.console.accounts`.** A plugin that matched a member to their account at a messenger says so: `put({ personId, messenger, workspace, account })`, `remove({ personId, messenger, workspace })` and `list({ messenger, workspace })`. An administrator consents to it by name, since an agent account takes a supervisor's own word at the messenger as an instruction by it; no other capability opens it. What a plugin said counts only while the plugin is enabled and holds the capability, no plugin says over another (`plugin.conflict`), and every change is an event of the workspace under the plugin's name: `person.updated` with the field `account`, the `messenger`, the `workspace`, the `account` and `set`, whether the account is the member's now. A console older than this has no `ctx.console.accounts`: a plugin that runs on both asks whether it is there. `createTestContext` takes `console.accounts`.
- **`POST /api/v1/agents/me/writers`** answers an agent account who accounts at a messenger are to it: whether the console is told the accounts of that workspace at all, the member each is by login, their kind, and whether they supervise the account that asks, which is an administrator or anyone above it in the reporting line at any level. `administrator` and `line` say how, and the other way too: `line` is `manager`, `above`, `report`, `below` or `null`, where the member stands in the asker's line, a report of its own and anyone further below among them. `ConsoleClient.writers` asks it.
- **The `slack` wake source writes the answer into the wake's first line**, after the account's id: `from the member U0123ABCD (@kim in the console, a supervisor of yours: your manager)`, `(@nova in the console, an agent account, no supervisor of yours)`, `(@lee in the console, no supervisor of yours, reports to you)`, or `(no member the console knows, so no supervisor of yours)`. The line of each earlier message a wake carries says the same, and so does `console agent messages`; and the wake's last line, which names the other agent accounts in a conversation, says it of each of them, for the agent to judge by whether to answer. It is the source's own line, which the agent reads under its session's mark and which no word of a message reaches; the source asks by the id and the workspace the messenger's event names for the writer, and hears only an answer for what it asked. Where the console says nothing, one older than the question, one that does not answer within five seconds, or a workspace no plugin told it the accounts of, a wake reads as before.
- **A session's first words, the desk's template and the skill `running-an-agent`** say that this line tells whose the account is where the console knows it, and that the address the messenger keeps, or a supervisor's tell that names the account, tells it where the line says nothing. A desk written before keeps its sentence until its account writes the new one in.
- **For whoever builds on the package:** `WakeHost.writers` is there for a wake source to ask by, and a database migration adds `messenger_accounts`.
